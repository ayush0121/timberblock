Block puzzle strategy: 12 tips to score higher

Works for Timberblock and any 8×8 block puzzle. Ordered from the habits that matter most.

Play Timberblock free
The one rule: a block puzzle is lost to the piece you can't place, not the line you didn't clear. Every tip below protects space first and chases points second.

Protect your space

1. Always keep room for a 3×3 square

The 3×3 square is the piece that ends most games. Before each placement, check there is still an empty 3×3 area somewhere on the board. If there isn't, your next placement should create one.

2. Keep one row or column open for the long line

The five-long line needs a clear run of five. Keeping one edge row or column mostly empty gives it a home.

3. Fill from the edges and corners inward

Edges are the hardest cells to fill later, because fewer pieces fit against them. Use awkward pieces (corners, L-shapes) on the edges early, and keep the middle flexible.

4. Never leave single-square holes

A lone empty cell surrounded by blocks can only be filled by the single square, which is rare. One hole is a nuisance; three holes usually end the run.

Score more per move

5. Build toward two lines at once

One line is worth 20 points, two at once 60, three 120 and four 200. Clearing two lines together is worth three single clears, so it often pays to wait one move and set up a double.

6. Line up rows and columns that cross

A piece placed where an almost-full row meets an almost-full column clears both. Crossings are where the 120- and 200-point clears come from.

7. Keep the combo streak alive

Clearing on consecutive placements adds 25% per step of the streak. When a streak is running, prefer the small clear now over the big clear later.

8. Small pieces are tools, not filler

Hold off placing the single square and the two-long line when you can. They are the pieces that finish a nearly complete line, so spend them to trigger a clear.

Plan the whole tray

9. Look at all three pieces before placing one

The order matters. Place the biggest, least flexible piece first while there's still room, and keep the flexible ones for last.

10. Watch for faded pieces

Timberblock fades a piece that no longer fits anywhere. If one is faded, your next move should open space for it, ideally by clearing a line.

11. Don't build towers

Stacking pieces into one corner leaves tall, narrow gaps that only lines can fill. Keep the filled area low and wide.

12. In Professional mode, speed beats perfection

Leftover seconds turn into bonus points when you clear a level, and the clock is the real opponent. Make a good move quickly rather than the perfect move slowly.

Practise on the daily board

The daily puzzle deals everyone the same pieces, so you can compare your score with friends and see exactly where a different choice would have helped.
